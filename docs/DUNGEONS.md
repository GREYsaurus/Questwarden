# Dungeon quests

## One mapping

A step belongs to a dungeon when it carries `dungeon=<instance>`. That comes from one place: the guide compiler (`tools/src/GuideCompiler.cs`).

- **From data:** when the bundled QuestieDB data puts **every** objective of a quest inside one dungeon or raid, the compiler tags its accept, complete and turn-in steps itself. Objectives here are kill targets, objects, and the NPCs or objects that drop quest items.
- **By hand:** `[dungeon=...]` in the guide source wins, for quests the data cannot place (objectives without spawn data).
- **Not tagged:** quests with only some objectives inside an instance. These are listed for review in `docs/generated/<Target>/dungeon-mixed.csv`. Breadcrumbs that only send you to an entrance are not tagged either.

Raids count as instances for this purpose, so a raid-only quest is tagged and left out with the dungeons.

## Filtering

Who decides: a character that finished setup uses its own choice (questing only or selected dungeons, docs/SETUP.md); others use the settings profile's *Include Dungeon Quests* and dungeon switches.

- **Include Dungeon Quests** off, or the dungeon not selected: the tagged steps are left out.
- **A quest already in your log** is never dropped or marked done. While its dungeon is off, or after you skip its run (the `complete` step), its objective and turn-in are skipped with a reason; the turn-in comes back once the quest is complete. Turned-in quests count as done.
- **Completionist** routes every dungeon's quests regardless of this selection (docs/COMPLETIONIST.md).
- **Follow-up quests** that need an excluded dungeon quest are skipped with the reason "needs a dungeon quest", so no step waits on a quest you will not do (`Core/Engine.lua`, `NeedsExcluded`).

## Dungeon runs

Code: `addon/Core/DungeonRun.lua`.

**The note.** A dungeon quest's objective step (`complete`, tagged `dungeon=`) has two stages, worked out from where you are:
- **Outside the dungeon**, with the quest in your log and not complete, the step reads *Run <dungeon>: find a group*. Its note says to find a group (Looking for Group channel, your guild, or the meeting stone) and that it moves on when you enter. The arrow, world-map pin and minimap pin point to the dungeon's entrance.
- **Inside the dungeon**, the same step shows the quest's objectives. Leaving with the quest unfinished brings the note and the entrance arrow back.

The stages are not separate saved steps, so saved guide progress is unchanged. Entering never marks a quest complete.

**Completionist** turns each dungeon with open quests into a *dungeon* step with the same note and arrow; it ends when you enter. Inside, a dungeon segment lists that dungeon's quest objectives; leaving plans the route again.

**Which dungeon you are in.** The client says whether you are in an instance (`IsInInstance`). The map you are on (`C_Map`) gives an area id, which is matched against each dungeon's instance areas. This works in every client language. A client that gives no map inside an instance counts the dungeon of the step that sent you there.

**Dying inside.** When you release, the ghost's arrow points to that dungeon's entrance, not to the corpse (which has no outdoor position). It survives a reload as a ghost (`ns.char.deathDungeon`) and ends when you are alive again. Deaths in raids and battlegrounds still get a note only.

**Entrances.** `Database/DungeonEntrances.lua` per target, generated by `tools/research/dungeon_entrances.py` from QuestieDB's `support/Zones/dungeons.lua` (commit `8f55e69`, base Classic/TBC table). Classic Era: 19 of 19 listed dungeons; TBC: 34 of 34. Where a dungeon has several entrances (Blackrock Mountain from Searing Gorge and Burning Steppes, Uldaman, Stratholme, the Dire Maul wings), the nearest one is used. A dungeon without an entrance in the data gets the note without an arrow; no position is guessed. The positions are QuestieDB's and have not been checked in game.

## XP

The route audit's XP model (`tools/src/RouteAnalyzer.cs`) gives **no XP for dungeon quest turn-ins** and none for dungeon kills. Level gates and the "needs level" skips therefore hold for players who skip dungeons. Players who do the dungeons will be ahead of the model.
